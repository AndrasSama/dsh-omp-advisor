# Timeline & Chronology Mapping

Most investigative narratives are chronologies, and most errors hide in them: unsourced dates, reversed sequences, and gaps quietly papered over. A disciplined timeline built from primary records is both a verification tool and a story-structure test. This skill reviews how a chronology was built and what it conceals.

## Watch for
- Dates asserted without a source for each one.
- Mixed time zones or date formats creating false sequences.
- "Before/after" claims that reverse when checked against primary records.
- Gaps in the record treated as if nothing happened during them.
- Conflating the date an event occurred with the date it was reported or filed.
- Relying on memory-based dates when documents exist.
- Sequence shown but causal links asserted without examination.
- A timeline that quietly omits events inconvenient to the narrative.

## Best practices
- Build the master timeline from primary records: filings, emails, logs, minutes.
- Attach a source and a confidence level to every date entry.
- Normalize to one time zone and one date format; note the originals.
- Mark gaps explicitly and investigate what records might fill them.
- Distinguish event date, disclosure date, and document date in separate columns.
- Cross-check the sequence against at least one independent chronology.
- Test the narrative against the timeline: does the story hold in strict order?
- Version the timeline as new documents arrive; never silently edit it.

## Quick checklist
- [ ] Every date has a cited source.
- [ ] Time zones and formats are normalized.
- [ ] Event vs. disclosure vs. document dates are distinguished.
- [ ] Gaps are marked and assigned follow-up.
- [ ] Sequence was verified against independent records.
- [ ] No omitted events contradict the narrative.
- [ ] Causal claims were checked against strict chronology.
- [ ] The timeline is versioned and current.
