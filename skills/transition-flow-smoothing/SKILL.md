# Transition Flow Smoothing

Readers rarely abandon a story because of a bad fact; they leave when they get lost. Unbridged jumps between topics, times, and places — and connectors that assert relationships the story has not earned — break the reading contract. This skill reviews drafts for the connective tissue that keeps a reader oriented.

## Watch for
- Paragraph jumps that switch topic, time, or place without a bridge.
- Repeated sentence openings that make the prose feel list-like.
- Abrupt shifts between narrative scenes and data or background blocks.
- Missing chronology signposts ("earlier," "by then," "two days later").
- Transitions asserting a connection the story has not earned ("as a result").
- Reader disorientation after long quote blocks with no re-anchoring.
- Section breaks used where a sentence-level bridge would do.
- End-of-paragraph sentences that point nowhere, leaving the next paragraph to start cold.

## Best practices
- End paragraphs with a hook or concept the next paragraph picks up.
- Use explicit time and place markers whenever the scene shifts.
- Alternate sentence openings; avoid three consecutive subject-first constructions.
- Bridge scene-to-analysis shifts with a sentence that states the connection.
- Re-anchor the reader after long quotes or digressions.
- Read the draft aloud, or read only paragraph-first sentences, to hear the jumps.
- Prefer logical connectors that reflect real relationships, not decorative ones.
- Let section headers do transition work in long pieces.

## Quick checklist
- [ ] No unbridged topic, time, or place jumps.
- [ ] Sentence openings vary.
- [ ] Scene/analysis shifts are explicitly bridged.
- [ ] Chronology signposts exist at every shift.
- [ ] Connectors reflect real logical relationships.
- [ ] The reader is re-anchored after long quotes.
- [ ] A first-sentence read-through was performed.
- [ ] Headers carry transitions in long pieces.
