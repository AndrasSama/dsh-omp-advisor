# Bias Detection Audit

Bias in journalism is rarely a declared opinion; it lives in word choice, source selection, story placement, and the false symmetry of both-sidesism. An audit pass that inspects these mechanics catches slant that a normal edit misses. This skill structures that audit for any draft.

## Watch for
- Loaded nouns and verbs that assign blame or virtue ("regime," "bureaucrat," "admitted").
- Framing that makes one side the default and the other the deviation.
- Source lists drawn entirely from one institution, ideology, or side of a dispute.
- Both-sidesism: pairing settled fact with unsupported denial as if equivalent.
- Placement and length that signal editorial judgment (burying inconvenient news).
- Selective detail: vivid negatives about one side, sanitized positives about the other.
- Headlines carrying a judgment the body does not support.
- Absence of affected communities from their own story.

## Best practices
- Run a language pass hunting evaluative words outside quotes; replace or attribute them.
- Audit the source roster: count who speaks, in what role, and with what framing.
- Weight evidence by quality, not by symmetry; do not manufacture a second side.
- Give proportional prominence: lead with what the evidence supports.
- Include affected people as sources, not just officials and experts.
- Check that headlines, captions, and photos carry no unstated judgment.
- Have someone outside the reporting team review for blind spots.
- Document why each contested framing choice was made.

## Quick checklist
- [ ] No unattributed evaluative language in body, hed, or caption.
- [ ] The source roster spans roles and perspectives proportionally.
- [ ] False balance avoided: evidence quality drives weighting.
- [ ] Story placement matches the significance of findings.
- [ ] Affected communities are represented as sources.
- [ ] Visuals were checked for framing bias.
- [ ] An independent second reader reviewed the draft.
- [ ] Contested framing choices are documented.
