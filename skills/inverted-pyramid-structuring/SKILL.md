# Inverted Pyramid Structuring

The inverted pyramid remains the workhorse structure of hard news because it serves readers, editors, and platforms at once: the most important verified facts come first, and every paragraph after is less essential. It is also the test of whether a reporter knows what their story actually is. This skill reviews structure for significance ordering and cuttability.

## Watch for
- The core finding appearing after paragraphs of background or scene-setting.
- Chronological storytelling where significance order would serve readers better.
- Paragraphs that cannot be cut from the bottom without losing essential facts.
- Key numbers, names, or outcomes buried mid-story.
- A second lede halfway down that restarts the story.
- Background dumped in one block instead of woven in as needed.
- Conclusions or implications saved for a kicker ending.
- A nut graf missing or arriving too late in hard-news pieces.

## Best practices
- Open with the most newsworthy verified fact: what happened, to whom, and why it matters.
- Order subsequent paragraphs by descending importance, not chronology.
- Front-load each paragraph with its own key point.
- Weave background in at the point of need, in small doses.
- Ensure any paragraph from the bottom can be deleted without breaking the story.
- Save color and anecdote for after the reader has the essentials.
- Keep hard-news and feature structures distinct; do not hybridize by accident.
- Test by cutting the story by a third from the bottom: does it still stand?

## Quick checklist
- [ ] The core news is in the first one or two paragraphs.
- [ ] Paragraphs run in descending importance.
- [ ] No essential fact sits in the bottom third.
- [ ] Background is woven in, not dumped.
- [ ] The story survives a one-third bottom cut.
- [ ] No buried second lede.
- [ ] A nut graf is present and early where required.
- [ ] Structure matches the genre (hard news vs. feature).
