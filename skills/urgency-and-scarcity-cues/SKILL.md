# Urgency and Scarcity Cues

Urgency and scarcity work because they make the cost of waiting concrete — and they backfire catastrophically when fake, because readers check.
Reviewing these cues means verifying every deadline, count, and limit against reality: is there a real mechanism enforcing it, can the reader verify it, and does the claim comply with consumer protection rules (FTC Act §5, EU unfair-commercial-practices rules)?

## Watch for
- Fake countdown timers that reset on refresh or restart every session (evergreen timers presented as real deadlines)
- "Only 3 left" stock claims not tied to actual inventory data
- "Limited time" with no end date, or an end date that silently rolls over
- Scarcity claims on digital goods with unlimited supply and no genuine capacity constraint
- Urgency copy with no reason WHY the deadline exists (price goes up, cohort closes, bonus expires)
- Dark patterns: hidden costs revealed at checkout, forced continuity, confirm-shaming ("No, I hate saving money")
- Stacked false cues: timer + fake stock + fake "27 people viewing" simultaneously

## Best practices
- Only deploy urgency with a real enforcement mechanism: an actual server-enforced deadline, real inventory counts, true cohort caps
- Always give the reason: "enrollment closes Friday because the cohort starts Monday" — the reason makes the deadline credible
- Make limits verifiable: live stock numbers, published end dates, explicit bonus expiry
- Use evergreen timers honestly: per-user deadlines (e.g., 48h from first visit) disclosed as such, not disguised as global events
- One cue per page; stacking reads as desperation and erodes trust
- Keep opt-outs and declines respectful — no confirm-shaming button copy
- Flag anything unverifiable: false urgency is prohibited by FTC and EU consumer-protection rules, not just bad practice

## Quick checklist
- [ ] Every deadline enforced by a real mechanism, not just copy
- [ ] Reason for the deadline stated
- [ ] Stock/seat counts sourced from real data
- [ ] Evergreen timers disclosed as per-user, if used
- [ ] One urgency cue per page, not stacked
- [ ] No confirm-shaming or hidden-cost dark patterns
- [ ] Claims would survive FTC/EU consumer-protection scrutiny
