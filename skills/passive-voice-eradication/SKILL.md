# Passive Voice Eradication

In accountability reporting, passive voice is more than a style flaw — it is where responsibility goes to hide. "Mistakes were made" names no one. The fix is not a blanket ban but a discipline: for every passive, ask who did it, and if the actor is known, put them in the sentence. This skill reviews drafts for hidden actors and for the passives that should stay.

## Watch for
- Agentless passives on consequential acts ("mistakes were made," "the funds were moved").
- Passives that obscure responsibility in accountability reporting.
- Bureaucratic passive clusters that drain agency from every sentence.
- Passives used to dodge attribution when the actor is known.
- Awkward active rewrites that change meaning in the rush to fix.
- Passive attribution where the speaker matters ("it was said that").
- Hidden actors in headlines, where the omission misleads the most readers.
- Confusing passive voice with past tense or stative verbs ("he was tired" is fine).

## Best practices
- Ask "who did it?" for every passive; if the actor is known, make it the subject.
- Keep passive when the recipient of the action is the story ("the mayor was indicted").
- Keep passive when the actor is genuinely unknown or unimportant.
- Prefer active attribution: "the department said," not "it was said by the department."
- Fix headlines first: hidden actors there mislead the largest audience.
- Rewrite sentence by sentence, checking that meaning and emphasis survive.
- Vary rhythm deliberately; not every sentence must be subject-verb-object.
- Flag systematic passivity to the reporter as a sourcing problem, not just a prose one.

## Quick checklist
- [ ] Every passive was checked for a known, nameable actor.
- [ ] Accountability-critical sentences are active.
- [ ] Legitimate passives (recipient focus, unknown actor) were retained.
- [ ] Attribution uses active constructions.
- [ ] Headlines contain no hidden actors.
- [ ] Rewrites preserve the original meaning.
- [ ] Sentence rhythm varies naturally.
- [ ] Systematic passivity was escalated as a sourcing issue.
