# Market Trend Analysis

Market-trend work sizes opportunities, identifies drivers, and projects category trajectories. In review, the discipline is evidentiary: every statistic needs a named, dated source, and every projection needs stated assumptions and bounds. Narrative momentum is not a substitute for data.

## Watch for
- TAM/SAM/SOM figures cited without source, methodology, or as-of date.
- Growth rates extrapolated linearly from short or anomalous base windows (pandemic spikes, launch quarters).
- Correlation presented as causation in trend-driver stories (e.g., "social media drove the uptake").
- Survivorship or selection bias in the comparable-company or case-study set.
- Vague attribution ("industry analysts say") without naming the research firm or report.
- Base-rate neglect: ignoring category saturation, cyclicality, or demographic ceilings.
- Nominal and real growth mixed without stripping inflation; currencies mixed without FX treatment.
- Point forecasts with no scenario bounds, confidence ranges, or sensitivity to key drivers.

## Best practices
- Require a named source and publication date for every market statistic; treat undated numbers as unverifiable.
- Triangulate load-bearing claims with at least two independent sources and note divergence.
- State the base year, the CAGR formula, and the projection horizon explicitly for every growth figure.
- Use base/bull/bear scenarios with named drivers instead of single-point forecasts.
- Distinguish secular trends from cyclical ones and state where the market sits in its cycle.
- Normalize for inflation and FX when comparing across periods or regions; label nominal vs real.
- Flag whether each trend claim is consensus or contrarian, and what evidence would falsify it.

## Quick checklist
- [ ] Every statistic has a named, dated source.
- [ ] CAGR/forecast formulas and horizons are stated.
- [ ] Scenarios or ranges accompany point forecasts.
- [ ] Nominal vs real and currency bases are labeled.
- [ ] Trend-driver claims are causal and evidenced.
- [ ] Anomalous base periods are acknowledged or excluded.
- [ ] Contrarian claims are explicitly flagged.
