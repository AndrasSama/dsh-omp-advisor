# Deepfake Disclosure Rules

Article 50 of the EU AI Act requires transparency for certain AI outputs: chatbots must disclose AI interaction, and synthetic or manipulated image/audio/video content must be disclosed and marked machine-readably. Review checks both layers — what users see and what survives platform processing — plus the consent and rights issues deepfakes of real people raise.

## Watch for
- AI-generated or manipulated image/audio/video published without disclosure where Article 50 applies.
- Disclosure only in metadata that platforms routinely strip — no user-visible disclosure.
- Machine-readable marking absent or non-interoperable (Article 50(2) requires marking in a machine-readable format).
- Chatbots not disclosing their AI nature to users (Article 50(1)).
- Emotion-recognition or biometric-categorization systems not informing the exposed persons (Article 50(3)).
- Deepfakes of real persons without consent — compounding image-rights and defamation exposure beyond disclosure duties.
- Satire/parody or editorial context unconsidered where disclosure rules interact with expression rights — jurisdiction-dependent nuance.
- No pipeline process to detect and label AI content consistently.

## Best practices
- Apply Article 50 duties by content type: chatbot disclosure, synthetic-content marking, emotion-recognition notification.
- Provide both user-visible disclosure and machine-readable marking (watermark, metadata) that survives typical distribution.
- Disclose at first interaction or exposure, not buried in terms.
- For deepfakes of real persons, assess consent, image rights, and defamation exposure in addition to disclosure.
- Document the marking technique and its robustness to cropping, compression, and re-encoding.
- Build detection and labeling into the content pipeline so disclosure is systematic, not ad hoc.
- Note jurisdiction-specific rules beyond the AI Act (national deepfake laws, platform policies, election-specific rules).
- Keep disclosure records for accountability and audit.

## Quick checklist
- [ ] Article 50 content type identified.
- [ ] User-visible disclosure present.
- [ ] Machine-readable marking applied.
- [ ] Disclosure at first exposure.
- [ ] Consent/rights checked for real persons.
- [ ] Marking robustness documented.
- [ ] Pipeline labeling process exists.
